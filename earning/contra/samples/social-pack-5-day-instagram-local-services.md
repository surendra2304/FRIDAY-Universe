# Sample Social Pack — 5 Days of Instagram for a Local Services Business

**Purpose:** Portfolio sample for the CometWorks Contra profile (profile-pack.md §5, item 3).
**Blurb on the profile:** "Five days of Instagram captions with hashtag sets and calendar placement, written for a local services business. Full packs run to 30 days in the same format."
**Format:** Five Instagram posts, Monday–Friday, for BrightNest Home Cleaning — a fictional local home-cleaning service used here as the example business. Each day carries a posting time, a hook-first caption with a call to action, a hashtag set matched to the niche and a local account, and a visual suggestion. The business, the town and every result are illustrative; no reviews, follower counts, bookings or guarantees are claimed anywhere.
**How to read it:** the five days rotate through five content pillars — proof, value, education, trust, offer — so a week never reads as five adverts in a row. Hashtag sets mix niche tags (people searching for the service), local tags (people searching near the business) and reach tags (people browsing the topic). `#[YourTown]` is a placeholder the client replaces with their real town; the rest of each set is ready to post. A 30-day pack repeats this rotation with fresh angles — same pillars, same format, no repeated captions.

## The week at a glance

| Day | Post | Time | Pillar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Before / after carousel | 7:30 am | Proof — show the result |
| Tuesday | The 10-minute reset (tip graphic) | 12:30 pm | Value — teach something useful |
| Wednesday | Myth vs reality | 7:30 am | Education — answer the objection |
| Thursday | Meet the team | 6:00 pm | Trust — the people behind the service |
| Friday | Weekend slots — book now | 5:00 pm | Offer — the week's one direct ask |

Times assume a local audience scrolling before work, at lunch, and after dinner. A client in a different timezone or with an evening-heavy audience shifts the slots; the rotation stays the same.

## Day 1 — Monday, 7:30 am — Before / after carousel

**Visual:** Carousel, 2–3 slides. Slide 1: the kitchen mid-clean (real mess, no staging). Slide 2: the same kitchen finished. Slide 3 (optional): the product caddy — what was used, nothing branded that the client doesn't actually use.

**Caption (92 words)**

This kitchen took 90 minutes. Swipe to see minute zero.

No filter, no staging — that first photo is what a family kitchen looks like on a Thursday night. The second is the same room after a BrightNest deep clean: hob degreased, floors mopped, every surface wiped down and put back where it belongs.

If your kitchen looks more like slide 1 than slide 2 right now, you're not behind. You're just due a deep clean.

Save this for your spring-clean list — or tap the link in bio to book one.

**Hashtags (10)**

#housecleaning #deepclean #cleaningservice #beforeandafter #cleanhome #cleaningmotivation #homeorganization #localbusiness #[YourTown] #smallbusinessowner

**Why this works:** proof before promises. The before photo does the selling — it looks like the reader's own kitchen, which is exactly why they stop scrolling. The caption names a real time (90 minutes) without inventing a price or a result, and the call to action is soft: save first, book second.

## Day 2 — Tuesday, 12:30 pm — The 10-minute reset

**Visual:** Single graphic or Reel, text on screen: "The 10-minute reset". Steps appear one at a time — surfaces, floors, bins, done. Filmed in one real home, in one take, no voiceover needed.

**Caption (102 words)**

The 10-minute reset that keeps a house tidy all week.

Set a timer. Ten minutes, four jobs, every evening:

1. Clear every flat surface — everything gets a home or a bag.
2. One room, one quick vacuum or sweep.
3. Bins out, fresh bags in.
4. Tomorrow's clothes laid out, kitchen sink empty.

That's it. Ten minutes tonight saves you the Saturday-morning avalanche — and it's the same order our cleaners work in, just scaled down for one person with a timer.

Save this for tonight. Try it once and tell us in the comments — did the timer beat you?

**Hashtags (10)**

#cleaningtips #homehacks #tidyhome #cleanhome #cleaningmotivation #homeorganization #momlife #busymum #[YourTown] #cleaningservice

**Why this works:** value first, no ask. A useful tip earns the save, and saves are what Instagram shows to more people — the post works for the account even though it sells nothing. The comment question ("did the timer beat you?") invites replies without begging for engagement. The service is mentioned once, as credibility, not as a pitch.

## Day 3 — Wednesday, 7:30 am — Myth vs reality

**Visual:** Two-panel graphic. Left: "MYTH — a deep clean takes all day." Right: "REALITY — most family homes take 2–3 hours with a two-person team." Plain background, big text, readable without zooming.

**Caption (104 words)**

Myth: a deep clean takes all day, costs a fortune, and means strangers in your house from dawn to dusk.

Reality: most family homes take two to three hours with a two-person team. You don't need to be there — plenty of our clients leave a key and come home to it done. And a deep clean isn't a luxury add-on; it's the reset that makes the weekly clean actually work, instead of wiping around the same dust for another month.

What else have you heard about cleaners that sounds too bad to be true? Ask us in the comments — we'll answer honestly.

**Hashtags (10)**

#cleaningmyths #deepclean #housecleaning #cleaningservice #homeorganization #cleanhome #localbusiness #smallbusiness #[YourTown] #cleaningtips

**Why this works:** it answers the objection before the sales post does. Price and time are the two reasons people don't book a cleaner; this post takes both head-on without naming a figure the business can't honour. The question at the end turns objections into content — every answer in the comments is next week's post idea.

## Day 4 — Thursday, 6:00 pm — Meet the team

**Visual:** Photo of two cleaners in uniform, smiling, in a finished living room — not stock, not staged in a studio. Caption names them by first name only. (If the client prefers no faces, the same caption works over a photo of the team's hands at work or the van.)

**Caption (85 words)**

The people who get a key to your house.

This is Priya and Sam — they've cleaned together for two years, they know which houses have the dog that steals socks, and they're the reason most of our clients book the same team every time.

Every BrightNest cleaner is background-checked and trained in our own homes before they ever clean yours. We'd rather grow slowly with people we trust than quickly with people we don't.

Say hello in the comments — they read every one.

**Hashtags (10)**

#meettheteam #localbusiness #smallbusiness #cleaningservice #housecleaning #supportsmallbusiness #shoplocal #[YourTown] #cleaningcompany #teamwork

**Why this works:** trust is the product. A cleaning service sells access to someone's home, so the face behind the uniform matters more than any offer. First names and one specific detail (the sock-stealing dog) make the team real in a way "professional, reliable staff" never does. The vetting line is written as the client would confirm it — see the note at the end.

## Day 5 — Friday, 5:00 pm — Weekend slots, book now

**Visual:** Single photo — a finished living room in evening light, or a flat-lay of the cleaning caddy by the front door. Calm image, no red "SALE" graphics, no countdown timers.

**Caption (89 words)**

Weekend slots are open — message us to hold yours.

Friday is when the week catches up with you. The kitchen from Monday, the bathroom you meant to do on Wednesday, the floors that have seen a full week of life.

Book a Saturday clean and come home to it done. Message us here or tap the link in bio — tell us how many bedrooms and bathrooms, and we'll confirm a time tonight.

One direct ask a week. That's the deal — the other four days earned it.

**Hashtags (10)**

#weekendclean #deepclean #housecleaning #cleaningservice #booknow #cleanhome #freshstart #localbusiness #[YourTown] #smallbusinessowner

**Why this works:** the offer lands on Friday, when the reader feels the week's mess and has the weekend in front of them — and it is the only hard ask in five posts, so it doesn't read as a business that only ever sells. No fake scarcity ("only 2 slots left") and no invented discount: the urgency is the reader's own weekend, not a countdown the business can't back up.

## A note on the numbers and the claims

- Caption lengths were measured on the caption text as written (92 / 102 / 104 / 85 / 89 words). Hashtag sets are 10 tags each — inside Instagram's usable range, with niche, local and reach tags mixed in every set rather than stacked at the end of the week.
- Nothing here invents a result. No review, rating, follower count, booking figure or "before/after" claim appears; the before/after is presented as a photo the business would supply, and the time figures ("90 minutes", "two to three hours") are the kind of working figures a real cleaner states from experience — a client replaces them with their own.
- Two lines are written as the client would confirm them, not as facts about a real business: the Day 4 vetting line ("background-checked and trained in our own homes") and the Day 1/Day 3 time figures. In a paid pack, those lines are built from the client's real facts in a short onboarding message — never guessed.
- `#[YourTown]` is the only placeholder in the file. Everything else posts as written. The 30-day version of this pack keeps the same five pillars and the same Monday–Friday rhythm, with fresh angles each week — six rotations, no repeated captions, one direct ask per week.

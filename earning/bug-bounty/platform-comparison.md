# Bug-Bounty Platforms — Comparison (free-only facts)

All four platforms are free for researchers: no signup fee, no subscription
required to hunt public programmes. Platforms earn from the companies running
programmes, not from researchers. Figures below are the platforms' public models
as of November 2026; programme-level details always come from each programme's own
page — read it before hunting (see `scope-and-legal-rules.md`).

## HackerOne (hackerone.com)

- **Signup:** free hacker account, email verification; identity verification is
  required before payouts, not before hunting most public programmes.
- **Programmes:** the largest directory — public programmes from startups to
  major tech companies, plus invite-only ones that unlock with reputation.
- **Bounty model:** per-report, set by each programme (typical public range
  $50–$10,000+ by severity; beginners realistically land $50–$500 first).
  Some programmes are vulnerability-disclosure only (no bounty) — filter those
  out when income is the goal.
- **Payouts:** PayPal or bank transfer, arranged inside the platform.
- **Beginner fit:** best starting point — public scope pages are standardised,
  and the platform's disclosed-reports archive is a free education in what valid
  reports look like.
- **Extras:** HackerOne's free "CSEC" / hacker curriculum material and the public
  Hacktivity feed (real disclosed reports) are required reading in the 30-day plan.

## Bugcrowd (bugcrowd.com)

- **Signup:** free researcher account; some programmes require identity checks or
  background screening (mainly US financial programmes) — skip those at first.
- **Programmes:** strong in retail, finance and SaaS; runs both public and
  private (invite) programmes; reputation ("accuracy") gates invites.
- **Bounty model:** per-report points + cash on bounty programmes; ranges similar
  to HackerOne, programme-set.
- **Payouts:** PayPal / Payoneer / bank via the platform.
- **Beginner fit:** good second platform. Bugcrowd University (free) is one of
  the best structured free introductions to web bug classes.
- **Extras:** its "Bug Bounty Forum" and disclosed reports are useful study
  material.

## Intigriti (intigriti.com)

- **Signup:** free researcher account; the platform vets researchers more
  (ID verification is commonly required before programme access).
- **Programmes:** Europe-heavy company base; many programmes are invite-only or
  capped-researcher, which means less duplicate competition once inside.
- **Bounty model:** per-report, programme-set; known for clear triage and
  consistent payouts in EUR.
- **Payouts:** bank transfer / platform-arranged.
- **Beginner fit:** second-phase platform — join after the first valid report
  elsewhere, when the vetting is easy to pass and invites start arriving.

## YesWeHack (yeswehack.com)

- **Signup:** free hunter account; European platform, GDPR-native.
- **Programmes:** public programmes from EU companies and public-sector bodies,
  plus private ones; smaller directory than HackerOne, also less crowded.
- **Bounty model:** per-report, programme-set, EUR-denominated commonly.
- **Payouts:** bank transfer via the platform.
- **Beginner fit:** also second-phase; its public programmes are a good
  low-competition hunting ground once the basics are proven in labs.

## Verdict for CometWorks

Start on **HackerOne** (directory size + disclosed-report education) with
**Bugcrowd** as the parallel second account (free training + extra programmes).
Add **Intigriti** and **YesWeHack** after the first valid report. Practise only
on **PortSwigger Academy** and **Hack The Box free tier** during the 30-day plan —
never on live sites outside a programme's scope.

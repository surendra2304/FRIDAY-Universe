## Episode description (paste into the podcast host)

Quoting a first freelance project usually means saying a number, regretting it,
and resenting the work. Priya Nair replaces the guess with a three-number
worksheet — your floor (one real month of costs divided by the days you can
honestly bill), your time (every step including the invisible work, plus a
buffer), and your risk (a new client, an unwritten brief, a floating deadline) —
then gives you the one sentence that states the price and stops. For freelancers
in their first two years quoting work this week.

## Timestamps

- **0:00** — Cold open: the price you say, then regret
- **1:10** — Welcome: three numbers, no pep talk
- **2:10** — Number one, your floor: real costs over billable days — the walk-away line
- **4:00** — Numbers two and three, time and risk: list every step, price the risk
- **6:20** — Saying the number out loud: state the price, then stop talking
- **7:40** — Sponsor message
- **8:00** — Listener question: when your floor is above your city
- **9:00** — Outro: homework — work out your floor rate

## Three takeaways

1. **Price from your floor, not from hope:** divide one real month of costs by
   the days you can honestly bill — that daily figure is your walk-away line,
   never your quote.
2. **Quote the film, not the poster:** list every step including the invisible
   work, add a quarter again, then add risk for a new client, an unwritten
   brief, or a floating deadline.
3. **Say the price, then stop:** "For the full project, it's [price], with half
   up front and half on delivery" — and if they push back, shrink the scope,
   never the rate.

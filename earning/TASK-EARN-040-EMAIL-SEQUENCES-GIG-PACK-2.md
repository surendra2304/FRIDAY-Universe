## 2. Sequence 1 — 5-email nurture (Standard tier, full)

**Fictional client:** June & Field, a small-batch candle studio (two founders, one workshop).
**Offer:** free scent guide (PDF) + 10% off a first order. **Goal:** turn new subscribers
into first-time buyers. **Send cadence:** immediately, day 2, day 4, day 6, day 9.
**Voice:** warm, plain, no hype. Every email: one idea, one call to action.

### Email 1 — Welcome + the free guide (send immediately)

- Subject options: "Your free scent guide is inside" / "Welcome to June & Field"
- Body:

> Hi — welcome in. Your free scent guide is linked below: six scents, what each one
> actually smells like, and which room each suits best.
>
> [Get the free scent guide]
>
> Pour a cup of something warm, take five minutes, and find your scent. Reply and tell
> me which one you picked — I read every reply.
>
> — June

### Email 2 — Value: make a candle last (send day 2)

- Subject options: "The 10-minute trick that doubles a candle's life" / "Why your candle tunnels (and the fix)"
- Body:

> Quick one. Most candles tunnel because the first burn is too short. Let the whole top
> melt before you blow it out — about an hour for our small jars — and the candle burns
> evenly to the bottom.
>
> Trim the wick to 5 mm before each light. That's it. Two habits, twice the burn time.
>
> The guide has the full care notes if you want them: [Get the free scent guide]
>
> — June

### Email 3 — Story: how the studio started (send day 4)

- Subject options: "We started in a kitchen with one pot" / "Why we pour in small batches"
- Body:

> June & Field started in our kitchen, one pot, one scent — cedar and smoke, poured for
> friends. People kept asking for more, so we kept pouring. Three years on, every candle
> is still poured by hand, in batches of forty, in the same workshop.
>
> Small batches mean we can change a scent when it isn't right, and we do. Nothing ships
> that we wouldn't burn at home.
>
> — June

### Email 4 — Proof + the question everyone asks (send day 6)

- Subject options: "Which scent sells out first? (It's not the one you'd guess)" / "The scent our repeat buyers pick"
- Body:

> The question I get most: which scent should I start with? The honest answer is our
> bestseller, Fig & Vetiver — warm, not sweet, and the one repeat buyers come back for.
>
> If you want a second opinion, reply with the room you're buying for and I'll tell you
> what I'd pick. No obligation — I like the puzzle.
>
> And if you're ready, your 10% welcome code is WELCOME10: [Shop the collection]
>
> — June

### Email 5 — Soft offer + goodbye-for-now (send day 9)

- Subject options: "Your 10% welcome code expires Sunday" / "One last thing before I go quiet"
- Body:

> Last email in this little series. Your 10% welcome code (WELCOME10) runs until Sunday
> night — after that it goes quiet, and I'll only write when there's something worth
> saying: a new scent, a restock, a workshop date.
>
> If a candle's been sitting in your cart, this is the nudge: [Shop the collection]
>
> Either way, thanks for reading. The guide is yours to keep.
>
> — June

## 4. Delivery notes (for the seller, not the buyer)

- Deliver each sequence as one formatted document: subject options + preview text (Premium)
  at the top of each email, send day in the margin, one CTA per email, links as
  placeholders the buyer replaces (`[Shop the collection]`, `[Get Golden Hour Presets — $39]`).
- Basic orders ship emails 1–3 of §2 only; Standard ships all of §2; Premium ships all of §3.
- Newsletter issues (Standard ×1, Premium ×2) are separate deliverables — write them from
  the buyer's current news, not from this pack.
- Never promise open, click or sales rates. Never invent testimonials, statistics or
  "limited spots" — the only deadline in this pack is a real cart-close date the buyer sets.

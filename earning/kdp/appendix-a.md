# Your First 100 Subscribers — Appendix A (Draft)

## Appendix A — Worksheets and Templates

This appendix collects, in one place, the worksheets and templates the
chapters promised — the same assets each chapter ended with, so you can copy
them without flipping back through the book. Fill them in by hand or on
screen. They work best completed in order: the Day-1 worksheet shapes the
lead magnet, the lead magnet shapes the signup page, and the signup page
shapes the welcome emails.

### A1. Day-1 avatar worksheet (Chapter 1)

Answer about ONE specific person — ideally a real person you know, have
served, or have been. Write in sentences, not labels.

1. My one person is: ____________________
   (What do they do all day? What are they trying to achieve this year that
   actually matters to them?)
2. What have they already tried that didn't work?
   ____________________
3. What do they complain about — in their own words, not marketing words?
   ____________________
4. When in their week does the problem hurt most?
   ____________________
5. What would a small, real win look like for them in the next 30 days?
   ____________________
6. The problem, in their words: ____________________
7. My one-sentence promise — *For [one person] who [one problem], this list
   sends [what], [how often], so they can [small real win]:*
   ____________________
8. The five people I talked to (or the evidence I used — comments, forum
   questions, honest 3-star reviews, my own messages from when I had the
   problem): ____________________

If you used the reconstruction route instead of five conversations, mark your
problem statement as a *draft* and revisit it after your first ten
subscribers, when real replies start correcting you.

### A2. Lead-magnet planner (Chapter 2)

One problem, one sitting. Your lead magnet solves one small piece of the
Day-1 problem completely — not the whole problem, and not five problems.

- **The one problem it solves:** ____________________
- **The one person it is for:** ____________________
  (Copy this from A1. If it does not match, stop and fix A1 first.)
- **Format — pick one:** checklist / template / mini guide / email course
- **Title — pick a formula and fill it in:**
  - *The [number]-point checklist for [small win]*
  - *The [thing] template: [result] without [pain]*
  - *How to [small win] in [one sitting / one afternoon]*
  - My title: ____________________
- **What the reader can do after using it that they could not do before:**
  ____________________
- **How long it takes the reader to use:** ____________________
  (If it takes longer than one sitting, cut it in half.)
- **What I will leave out:** ____________________
  (The rest of the problem belongs in the welcome emails and the weekly
  email — not in the magnet.)
- **Quality check — would a stranger trade an email address for this?**
  Yes / No — and the honest reason: ____________________
- **Free tools I will build it with:** ____________________
  (Check the current free tier before you promise yourself a tool does
  something for free.)

### A3. Signup-page copy template (Chapter 3)

One page. One field (the email address). One button. Fill in the blanks,
then cut every word that does not earn its place.

- **Headline — the promise from A1, shortened for a phone screen:**
  ____________________
- **Bullet 1 — what they get:** ____________________
- **Bullet 2 — how often:** ____________________
- **Bullet 3 — what it is not:** ____________________
  (For example: "No daily emails. No selling in every email.")
- **Button text:** ____________________
  (For example: "Send me the checklist" — name the magnet, not the list.)
- **Two-sentence privacy promise:**
  *I will only use your email address to send you [what you promised]. I
  will never share it or sell it, and you can unsubscribe at any time with
  one click.*
- **Mobile check before you publish:** read the whole page on your phone.
  Does the headline fit without scrolling sideways? Is the button reachable
  with one thumb? Does the form ask for anything besides the email address?
  If any answer is no, fix it before you invite anyone.

### A4. The five welcome-email skeletons (Chapter 4)

Send in order, one per day or spaced across the first week. Each email does
one job. The value-to-offer ratio across the sequence is four parts useful
help to one part invitation — the offer appears in Email 4, and nowhere else
as a pitch.

**Email 1 — Deliver + story.**
Subject: *[Your lead magnet] is here* / *Here's the [checklist/template/guide]
you asked for*
- Here is your [lead magnet]: [link or attachment].
- Start here: [the first step, in one sentence].
- Why I made this: [two or three sentences of your story — the problem you
  had, and what changed].
- Tomorrow I will send you [the next useful thing].

**Email 2 — Teach.**
Subject: *[One specific thing they will learn]*
- The most common mistake people make with [the problem]: [name it].
- Do this instead: [one concrete action, with an example].
- Try it today and see what happens. No reply needed — but I read them all.

**Email 3 — Teach + proof of concept.**
Subject: *[How it worked for one real person]*
- Here is what happened when [a real person — you, or a reader with
  permission] tried [the idea from Email 2]: [what they did, what changed].
- Be honest about what did not work, too: [the limit or the part that took
  longer than expected].
- Your turn: [the same action, adapted to the reader's situation].

**Email 4 — Invite.**
Subject: *[A question that invites a reply]*
- By now you have [what the reader has done: used the magnet, tried the
  idea]. The next step, if you want one, is [the small next step — a reply,
  a question, a closer look at their situation].
- If you would like that, just reply to this email. Tell me [the one thing
  you need to help them — for example: what you post about, or what you
  sell].
- No obligation — the weekly email continues either way.

**Email 5 — Ask a question.**
Subject: *[A short, genuine question]*
- I have one question for you: [the question — for example: "What is the
  hardest part of this for you right now?"]
- Reply with a sentence or two. I read every reply, and your answer decides
  what I write about next.
- Thank you for being here. The next email arrives on [day]: [what it will
  be about].

Keep each email to 300–600 words. Write like one person to one person —
because that is what it is.

### A5. Launch-post scripts (Chapter 6)

One post per platform, each naming the same person, the same problem, and
the same lead magnet. Adapt the words; keep the structure. Post once per
platform, then go and be useful in the comments instead of reposting.

**Script 1 — The direct invitation (your main platform).**
> I made something for [one person — for example: physiotherapists whose
> Instagram takes hours and brings in no patients]. It's a [lead magnet —
> for example: a short checklist] that helps you [small win — for example:
> turn one patient question into a week's worth of posts]. It's free. If
> that sounds like you, here's the link: [signup page link]. And if it's
> not for you, I'd be grateful if you shared it with someone it fits.

**Script 2 — The question (a group or community where you already take
part).**
> Quick question for [one person]: what's the hardest part of [the problem]
> right now? I'm putting together [the lead magnet] and I want it to solve
> the real problem, not the one I guessed. [Signup page link, only if the
> group's rules allow links — check first.]

**Script 3 — The short one (a second platform or your email signature).**
> [One-sentence promise from A1.] Get the free [lead magnet] here: [link].

Rules that travel with these scripts: ask your warm circle first, in person
or by message, before you post publicly. Follow each community's rules about
self-promotion — if links are not welcome, ask the question without one and
invite interested people to message you. And never post the same script twice
in the same place: a launch is an invitation, not a campaign.

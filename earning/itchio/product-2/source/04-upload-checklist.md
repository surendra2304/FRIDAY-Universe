# Document 04 — The Upload Checklist (27 Points)

Run this top to bottom for every upload. Each item exists because skipping it
measurably costs clicks, watch time or subscribers.

## Before you export

1. ☐ First 30 seconds re-watched: does the hook pay off the thumbnail's promise?
2. ☐ Audio levels even — no sudden loud/quiet patches; voice clear over any music.
3. ☐ Captions/subtitles checked on the full video (auto-captions corrected for
      names and key terms).
4. ☐ No copyrighted music/clips you don't have rights to (Content ID claims
      divert revenue and can block the video).
5. ☐ Export matches platform spec (1080p minimum; 4K upscale improves the codec
      YouTube assigns, if your machine handles it).

## Title & thumbnail

6. ☐ Title completes the thumbnail text instead of repeating it.
7. ☐ Title under ~60 characters so it isn't cut off on mobile.
8. ☐ Thumbnail passed the phone-size squint test.
9. ☐ Title contains the searchable phrase a stranger would type (front-loaded).

## Description

10. ☐ First two lines restate the video's promise with the main keyword (this is
       what search and suggested traffic read first).
11. ☐ Chapter timestamps added (they create search entry points mid-video).
12. ☐ Links: the one next video you want watched, then resources mentioned.
13. ☐ No link stuffing — 3–5 links maximum, each earning its place.
14. ☐ A plain-language summary paragraph for accessibility and search.

## Settings & metadata

15. ☐ Category and language set correctly (not left on defaults).
16. ☐ Tags: 5–10 specific phrases viewers actually search — your title phrase,
       2–3 close variants, your niche's core terms. (Tags matter less than they
       did; titles matter more. Don't burn an hour here.)
17. ☐ Playlist: video added to the right series playlist (playlists drive
       session time, which drives recommendations).
18. ☐ End screen: last 15 seconds point to ONE next video (the bridge your
       script promised), not four competing cards.
19. ☐ Cards: at most 2, placed at natural curiosity peaks, never in the first
       30 seconds.
20. ☐ Audience setting answered correctly (made-for-kids rules change comments
       and notifications — getting it wrong costs reach).
21. ☐ Visibility/schedule: publish at your audience's active hour (check your
       analytics' "when viewers are online" once you have data; until then,
       early evening in your audience's main timezone).

## Publish hour

22. ☐ Pinned comment posted immediately: a question or a "watch next" pointer —
       it seeds the comment section viewers judge the video by.
23. ☐ Community post / short teaser pointing to the video (if your channel has
       the tab).
24. ☐ Reply to every comment in the first hours — early engagement velocity is
       a real signal and early commenters become regulars.
25. ☐ Share once where your audience already is (one community, not ten spam
       posts).

## After publishing

26. ☐ At 24–48 hours, check the analytics trio: click-through rate (packaging),
       average view duration (script), traffic source (what to make next).
       Change nothing yet — one video is an anecdote.
27. ☐ Log the video in a simple tracker: topic, template used, hook used, CTR,
       AVD. After 10 videos, your own data beats every guru's advice — this
       tracker is how the Starter Pack turns into your channel's playbook.

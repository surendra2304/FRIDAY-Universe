# Part 1 — Resume rewrite template

*How to use it: work top to bottom. Each section has one job; if a section does not earn its place for this buyer, cut it — never pad it. Replace every `[BRACKETED]` item with the buyer's real facts before delivery. A 1-page resume (Basic) runs sections 1–4 + 6, tightened; the 2-page resume (Standard/Premium) runs all sections.*

## 1. Header / contact

*Purpose: name, target role, and one way to reach the buyer — nothing else. Length: 3–4 lines.*
*Writer's note: no photo, no date of birth, no full home address (city + region is enough). One phone number, one email, one LinkedIn URL. The target role sits directly under the name so the reader knows in two seconds what this person is for.*

> **[Full name]**
> [Target role — e.g. Logistics Coordinator]
> [City, Region] · [Phone] · [Email] · [LinkedIn URL]

## 2. Professional summary

*Purpose: three lines that answer "who is this, and what do they do for me?" Length: 40–60 words.*
*Writer's note: lead with years + field, then the one or two things the buyer is genuinely best at, then the roles they are targeting. No adjectives without evidence ("passionate", "dynamic", "results-driven" mean nothing on their own — the bullets below are the evidence). Never write a summary that could belong to anyone in the field.*

> [Years] [field] professional with [one concrete strength] and [second concrete strength]. [One line on the environments they have worked in, e.g. high-volume warehouse, agency, startup]. Now targeting [target roles].

## 3. Core skills / keywords

*Purpose: the ATS scan and the human scan in one place. Length: 8–12 items, one or two lines.*
*Writer's note: take the keywords from real postings for the target roles, not from a generic list — and only list skills the buyer actually has. Group them (Tools / Methods / Languages) when the list is long; a single comma-run is fine when it is short. Every skill here must appear, with evidence, in the experience section.*

> **Tools:** [tool 1], [tool 2], [tool 3]
> **Methods:** [method 1], [method 2]
> **Languages / other:** [item 1], [item 2]

## 4. Experience — the achievement bullet

*Purpose: prove the summary with results, not duties. Length: 3–5 bullets per role, most recent first.*
*Writer's note: every bullet follows one formula — **[strong verb] + [what you did] + [scale or result]**. "Responsible for X" is banned; say what changed because the buyer was there. Numbers come from the buyer's real records; where a number does not exist, use scale ("a team of 6", "400+ tickets a week") instead of inventing a result. One bullet per role may describe scope (team size, budget, volume) if the role needs context. Job titles and dates are the buyer's real ones, never polished into a different job.*

> **[Job title] — [Employer], [City] — [Month Year]–[Month Year]**
> - [Verb] [what] [for whom / at what scale], [result with a real number].
> - [Verb] [what], [result].
> - *(repeat 3–5 bullets; most recent role gets the most)*

## 5. Education & training

*Purpose: the credential, the institution, the year — and only the courses that matter for the target role. Length: 2–5 lines.*
*Writer's note: degree first, then relevant certifications and short courses. Drop the graduation year only if the buyer asks (age bias is real, and it is the buyer's call). No coursework lists, no grades unless they are exceptional and recent.*

> **[Qualification] — [Institution], [Year]**
> [Certification / short course relevant to the target role, Year]

## 6. Additional — only if it helps

*Purpose: one or two lines that add something a recruiter would actually use. Length: 1–3 lines, or omit.*
*Writer's note: languages, licences, portfolios, volunteer work — only if it supports the target role. Hobbies go here only when they are genuinely relevant (a designer's side project, a driver's clean licence record). If in doubt, omit.*

> [Languages, licences, portfolio link, or relevant extra — one line each.]

## 7. Cover letter structure (Standard / Premium)

*Purpose: the same story as the resume, told once, in the buyer's voice. Length: 250–300 words, 4 short paragraphs.*
*Writer's note: paragraph 1 names the role and the one-line reason the buyer fits it. Paragraph 2 gives the two strongest proofs from the resume, with numbers. Paragraph 3 says why this employer, specifically — one real fact about them, never a generic compliment. Paragraph 4 asks for the interview and says when the buyer is available. No repeating the resume bullet by bullet; no "I am writing to apply" filler.*

> **Para 1:** [Role] + [one-line fit].
> **Para 2:** [Proof 1 with a number] + [Proof 2 with a number].
> **Para 3:** [Why this employer — one real fact].
> **Para 4:** [Ask for the interview + availability].

# Part 2 — LinkedIn headline templates

*How to use it: the headline is 220 characters, and only the first ~60 show in search results — so the role and the value must land in the first line. Pick the formula that fits the buyer; never stack keywords with pipes and no meaning. Writer's note: write 3 options (Premium) and pick the sharpest; test it by reading it aloud — if it could belong to anyone with the same job title, rewrite it.*

## Formula 1 — Role + who you help + how

> [Role] helping [audience] [outcome] | [tool/skill 1] · [tool/skill 2]

*Example: Logistics Coordinator helping e-commerce teams ship on time, every time | WMS · route planning · supplier coordination*

## Formula 2 — Role + specialism + proof

> [Role] · [specialism] · [one real proof, with a number]

*Example: Brand Designer · packaging & identity for food brands · 40+ launches in 6 years*

## Formula 3 — Outcome first, role second

> [Outcome the hiring manager wants] | [Role] | [audience]

*Example: Turning messy sales data into decisions | Data Analyst | e-commerce & retail*

## Formula 4 — Transition (career changers)

> [New role] | ex-[previous field], bringing [transferable strength] to [new field]

*Example: Junior UX Designer | ex-teacher, bringing classroom-tested research skills to product teams*

## Formula 5 — Senior / specialist

> [Role] · [niche] · [years] years · [one thing you are known for]

*Example: Operations Manager · warehouse & fulfilment · 9 years · the person you call when peak season breaks*

# Part 3 — LinkedIn About (summary) templates

*How to use it: About allows 2,600 characters (~350–400 words). Aim for 200–300 words: long enough to tell a story, short enough to finish. All three structures below open with the buyer in their own words and close with what they are looking for and how to reach them. Writer's note: first person, plain language, no third-person bio voice. One concrete number or fact in the first 3 lines — the "see more" fold hides everything after line 3, so the hook must sit above it.*

## Structure A — Story (best for career changers and early career)

> **Para 1 (the hook, 2–3 lines):** [The moment or problem that pulled you into this work — specific, not generic.]
> **Para 2 (the proof, 3–5 lines):** [What you have done since, with one or two real numbers or facts.]
> **Para 3 (the now, 2–3 lines):** [What you do now / what you are looking for + how to reach you.]

## Structure B — Expertise (best for experienced hires)

> **Para 1 (the headline, 2–3 lines):** [Role + years + the one thing you are best at, with a number.]
> **Para 2 (the method, 3–5 lines):** [How you work — the approach, not a list of duties.]
> **Para 3 (the now, 2–3 lines):** [What you are looking for + how to reach you.]

## Structure C — Problem / solution (best for specialists and freelancers)

> **Para 1 (the problem, 2–3 lines):** [The problem your audience has, in their words.]
> **Para 2 (the fix, 3–5 lines):** [How you solve it — process and proof, with a real fact.]
> **Para 3 (the now, 2–3 lines):** [Who should contact you and for what + how to reach you.]

# Part 4 — LinkedIn experience entry template

*How to use it: LinkedIn experience is not a copy of the resume — it is shorter, more conversational, and it may say things a one-page resume cannot. 2–3 bullets per role, plus one line of context. Writer's note: keep the employer's real name, title and dates identical to the resume. Add media (portfolio link, project) only where the buyer has something real to show.*

> **[Job title] — [Employer] — [Month Year]–[Month Year]**
> [One line: what the team / operation did, for context.]
> - [Achievement bullet — same formula as the resume, conversational tone.]
> - [Achievement bullet.]
> **Skills:** [3–5 skills, matching the resume.]

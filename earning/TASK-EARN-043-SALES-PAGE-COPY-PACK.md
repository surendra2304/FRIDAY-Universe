# Part 1 — Long-form sales page template

*How to use it: work top to bottom. Each section has one job; if a section does not earn its place for this buyer, cut it — never pad it. Replace every `[BRACKETED]` item with the buyer's real facts before delivery. Sections marked ★ are Premium-only; the Standard page runs sections 1–9 + 11–13, the Basic refresh reworks the buyer's existing sections against this order.*

## 1. Eyebrow / pre-headline

*Purpose: tell the right reader "this is for you" in one line, before the headline. Length: 1 line.*
*Writer's note: name the reader and the outcome, not the product. "FOR FREELANCERS WHO DREAD TAX SEASON" beats "INTRODUCING OUR NEW COURSE".*

> FOR [READER]: [OUTCOME THEY WANT, IN THEIR WORDS]

## 2. Headline + sub-headline

*Purpose: stop the scroll and make the promise. The headline carries the outcome; the sub-headline carries how, and for whom. Length: headline 6–12 words, sub-headline 1–2 lines.*
*Writer's note: write 8 options (Premium) and pick the sharpest; never lead with the product name or a feature. Test the headline by reading it aloud to someone outside the niche — if they cannot repeat the promise back, rewrite it.*

> # [OUTCOME] — [WITHOUT THE PAIN THEY EXPECT]
>
> ## [How it works in one line], for [reader] who [their situation].

## 3. Opening hook / lead

*Purpose: show the reader you understand their exact problem, in their words, before you mention the offer. Length: 100–200 words.*
*Writer's note: open in the middle of their worst moment with the problem, not with your biography. Use "you" twice as often as "I/we". No claim you cannot evidence.*

> You know the [moment]. [Concrete scene, 2–3 sentences, sensory detail]. And [the frustrating result they keep getting].
>
> It is not [the thing they blame themselves for]. It is [the real, fixable cause]. This page is about [the fix, in one line].

## 4. The cost of staying stuck ★

*Purpose: make the status quo feel expensive — in time, money or stress — so doing nothing reads as a decision, not a default. Length: 80–150 words.*
*Writer's note: use the buyer's real numbers (hours lost, fees paid, opportunities missed). Never invent a cost figure; if the buyer has none, use time ("three more months of…") instead of money. No fear-mongering, no shame.*

> Every [week/month] this stays as it is costs you [real, evidenced cost]. Over a year, that is [honest arithmetic, shown]. The page does not have to stay like this — but nothing changes on its own.

## 5. The shift — why the usual advice fails

*Purpose: explain the mechanism. The reader has tried the obvious fixes; show why they failed and what works instead. This is the section that earns trust. Length: 150–300 words.*
*Writer's note: name 2–3 common fixes the reader has already tried and say plainly why each one falls short. Then state your mechanism in one sentence and prove it with process detail, not adjectives.*

> You have probably tried [fix 1], [fix 2] and [fix 3]. [Why each one fails — one line each, no straw men].
>
> The problem is not [effort / talent / budget]. The problem is [mechanism, one sentence]. So instead of [old approach], this [offer] does [new approach, concretely].

## 6. The offer — what it is, who it is for

*Purpose: introduce the offer only now, after the problem and mechanism are established. Length: 80–150 words.*
*Writer's note: one paragraph on what it is, one line on who it is for, one line on who it is NOT for. Saying who it is not for is the fastest trust-builder on the page.*

> **[Offer name]** is [what it is, in one sentence]. It is for [reader] who [situation]. It is not for [person it will not help] — if that is you, this page will save you the money.

## 7. What's inside — benefit-led breakdown

*Purpose: translate every feature into the outcome it buys. Length: 1 short paragraph per item, 4–8 items.*
*Writer's note: format is always "[Feature] — so you can [outcome]". Never list a feature without its "so you can". Order by the reader's priority, not by the order the buyer built them.*

> - **[Item 1] — so you can [outcome].** [One line of concrete detail.]
> - **[Item 2] — so you can [outcome].** [One line of concrete detail.]
> - *(continue for each item; include bonuses here, labelled as bonuses, with their real value or "included" — never an invented value.)*

## 8. How it works — 3 steps

*Purpose: make starting feel small. Length: 3 steps, 1–2 lines each.*
*Writer's note: step 1 must be doable in under 10 minutes. If step 1 sounds like work, the reader leaves.*

> 1. **[Step 1 — the easy start].** [What they do, in one line.]
> 2. **[Step 2 — the main work].** [What they do, in one line.]
> 3. **[Step 3 — the result].** [What changes, in one line.]

## 9. Proof

*Purpose: evidence the promise with things that are true. Length: 100–250 words.*
*Writer's note: use, in this order of strength — (a) the seller's credentials and track record, (b) the process (how many people, how long, what was measured), (c) the guarantee (section 11). Testimonials only if real, with a real name and context the buyer supplies. If the buyer has no proof yet, say so honestly and lean on process + guarantee; never fill the gap with invented quotes.*

> [Seller] has [credential / years / number of people helped, all real]. The [method] was built over [time] from [real source: client work, teaching, practice]. [One concrete, checkable fact about the method or the seller.]

## 10. Objections — FAQ ★

*Purpose: answer the exact doubts a careful buyer has, before they click away. Length: 4–6 questions, 2–4 lines each.*
*Writer's note: write the questions the buyer is actually afraid to ask ("What if it doesn't work for me?", "How is this different from free content?", "What if I fall behind?"). Answer directly; a hedged answer loses more than the objection did. Price objections are answered in section 11, not here.*

> **"What if [objection]?"**
> [Direct answer, 2–4 lines, with a fact where possible.]
>
> *(repeat for each objection)*

## 11. Price, value stack and guarantee

*Purpose: state the price after the value is established, then remove the risk. Length: 80–150 words plus the stack.*
*Writer's note: stack the real components with their real prices (or "included"); the total must be honest arithmetic. The guarantee must be one the seller will actually honour — 14 or 30 days, stated plainly, with what "refund" means. No countdown timers unless the deadline is real.*

> **[Offer name] — [price].**
>
> You get [component 1], [component 2], [component 3] — [honest total value if stated].
>
> **Guarantee:** [Plain-language guarantee: what happens, within how long, how to claim it.]

## 12. Honest urgency ★

*Purpose: give a real reason to act now, if one exists. Length: 1–3 lines, or omit entirely.*
*Writer's note: use only a true constraint — a real cohort date, a real price rise, a real capacity limit. If none exists, OMIT this section. Fake scarcity is the fastest way to lose a buyer and get a gig reported.*

> [Real constraint, stated plainly, with the date or number.] After that, [what actually happens].

## 13. Final CTA + what happens next

*Purpose: one action, and remove the fear of clicking it. Length: 50–100 words.*
*Writer's note: repeat the single action (buy / book / sign up) and describe exactly what happens after the click — what they receive, when, and how. End on the outcome, not the transaction.*

> **[CTA button copy: the outcome, e.g. "Start [outcome] today".]**
>
> When you [click / order], [exactly what happens next: what lands in their inbox, when, and the first thing they do]. [One-line restatement of the promise.]

## 14. P.S. lines ★

*Purpose: catch the skimmers who scrolled straight here. Length: 2–3 lines.*
*Writer's note: P.S. 1 restates the promise + guarantee; P.S. 2 names who it is not for (saves refunds); P.S. 3, only if true, restates the real deadline.*

> **P.S.** [Promise + guarantee in one line.]
> **P.P.S.** [Who it is not for, in one line.]
